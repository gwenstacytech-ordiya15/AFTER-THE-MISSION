What happens after a mission?

Spacecraft don't all end their journeys in the same way. Their final state depends on their destination, design, fuel, power and scientific goals.

01 · RemainHardware stays on a surface after its work is complete.
02 · RetireA spacecraft stops operating when power or systems run out.
03 · DisposeA spacecraft may be deliberately directed into a planet or other safe destination.
04 · ContinueA spacecraft may keep travelling after its main mission ends.
Important distinction: “Still travelling” is not the same as “abandoned.” Voyager 1 is still on its journey, while other spacecraft have ended operations.
The bigger picture

A mission has a lifecycle

From launch to its final chapter, every spacecraft follows a journey shaped by engineering decisions and scientific discoveries.

01

Launch

A rocket carries the spacecraft away from Earth and places it on its planned trajectory.

02

Explore

Instruments collect images, measurements and other data that help scientists answer questions.

03

Reach a limit

Fuel, power, hardware, communications or mission goals can limit how long a spacecraft operates.

04

Leave a legacy

Even after operations end, discoveries and engineering lessons continue to shape future missions.

From screen to shoebox

Build the mission model

Turn the website's story into a physical display. Divide a shoebox into three zones and show how spacecraft missions leave different kinds of legacies.

01 · THE MOON

Surface hardware

Make a grey lunar landscape with craters. Add a small Apollo Lunar Module descent stage and a Lunar Roving Vehicle. Label the equipment that remains on the Moon.

02 · MARS

Silent explorers

Use red-orange terrain, rocks and a rover. Include Opportunity and InSight, explaining how dust and power limitations contributed to the end of their operations.

03 · DEEP SPACE

Still travelling

Create a dark star field with Saturn and a tiny spacecraft. Show Cassini's planned end at Saturn, then Voyager 1 continuing beyond the planets.

Make the display tell a story

  • Give each zone a clear title and colour palette.
  • Add a small information card for every mission.
  • Use arrows to show where a spacecraft went or what happened to it.
  • Include a QR code linking to the website, if available.
  • Keep labels short enough to read at a glance.

Materials checklist

  • Shoebox and cardboard dividers
  • Paint, paper, glue and scissors
  • Modelling clay or recycled materials
  • Printed labels and mission facts
  • Small lights or reflective paper (optional)

Keep the model safe and lightweight. Ask an adult for help with any cutting tools or hot glue.

THE BIGGER PICTURE

A mission's final chapter

Spacecraft can leave very different legacies. Their endings are shaped by engineering, science and the environment they explore.

01 · RemainHardware stays on a surface after its work is complete.
02 · RetireA spacecraft stops operating when power or systems run out.
03 · DisposeA spacecraft is deliberately sent to a planned destination.
04 · ContinueA spacecraft keeps travelling beyond its original mission.
